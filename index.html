Case study published: Premium Residential Villa, Coimbatore — sloping plot, raft foundation, PT slabs, cantilevered first floor.

◆ 52 reactions◆ 11 comments

A four-bedroom family residence on a sloping plot in the northern corridor of Coimbatore. The brief asked for generous internal volumes, a strong relationship with the landscape and a plan that could work for a family over twenty years — not just for the handover photograph. The design arranges living volumes around a double-height courtyard that is open to the west, with the service and utility rooms forming a solid edge to the north for privacy and thermal control.

A flat-roofed contemporary composition with deep overhangs, a vertical fin screen on the solar-facing elevation and a continuous stone datum that ties the internal floor levels to the landscape. Interior planning separates a double-height living volume, a formal dining court and a service core, so that circulation never crosses the principal rooms.

  • Double-height living volume with a cantilevered first floor slab
  • West-facing courtyard with a reflecting pool and shaded colonnade
  • Vertical fin screen sized for the site's solar gain
  • Service core acting as a thermal buffer to the north edge

The plot fell approximately 1.4 m across the site and the earlier exploratory bores showed a thin lateritic layer over weathered rock. A previous owner had filled the southern portion with loose granular material, which meant the founding depth varied sharply across a very short distance. Access was restricted by an existing compound wall, so all material movement and crane positioning was planned around a single gate position.

  • 1.4 m fall across the plot with a constrained single-gate access
  • Variable founding depth: fill, laterite, then weathered rock
  • High water table observed at the lowest corner
  • Existing compound wall retained and incorporated into the landscape

Three problems had to be solved before the frame could be detailed: how to found the structure safely in variable ground, how to hold a level floor plate on a sloping site, and how to carry a cantilevered first floor without a visual prop. Each was resolved at design stage rather than on site, which is where programmes are usually lost.

  • Selecting a foundation type across three different ground conditions
  • Achieving a level plinth without extensive filling and long-term settlement risk
  • Balancing a 3.6 m cantilever against deflection and vibration limits
  • Coordinating the pool, the courtyard and the retaining edges with the frame grid

The raft was cast in three pours with a construction joint planned to coincide with the structural grid lines, allowing the transfer beam and cantilever to be cast monolithically with the slab above. The lower level used a suspended ground floor on backfill with a post-tensioned slab to keep the plinth level while removing the differential settlement risk of a large fill.

  • Three-pour raft with construction joints aligned to the grid
  • Post-tensioned ground slab replacing deep fill across the plot
  • Monolithic casting of transfer beam, cantilever and slab
  • Weekly cube testing and a photographic inspection record per pour

M40 concrete with a 12% minimum cement content was specified for the transfer zone and the ground slab. High-strength steel was used for the cantilever reinforcement, with the top layer of the transfer beam detailed as continuous over the supporting columns. Non-shrink grout was used below all steel base plates and holding-down bolts.

M40 (transfer & ground slab)
Reinforcement

Founding

The completed building was photographed across two sessions: a golden-hour exterior pass covering the courtyard, pool and west elevation, and a controlled interior session in the evening to capture the lighting design without ambient contamination. The image set is graded to a single visual standard so it can run across the website, Instagram, LinkedIn and print without rework.

Project progress timeline

Sample programme for a 14-month build.

Phase 01 · Months 1–2

Site investigation & design development

Exploratory bores, topographical survey, structural options study and the frozen foundation design.

Phase 02 · Months 3–5

Earthworks, piling support & raft

Bulk cut and fill, dewatering, reinforcement fixing and the three-pour reinforced raft with tested concrete.

Phase 03 · Months 5–7

Ground floor & retaining works

Post-tensioned ground slab, courtyard retaining walls and the permanent drainage network installed and tested.

Phase 04 · Months 7–11

Superstructure & envelope

Columns, cantilever transfer beam, first-floor slab, block masonry, waterproofing and façade frames.

Phase 05 · Months 11–13

MEP, finishes & landscape

Services first-fix and second-fix, flooring, joinery, pool and landscape works, external lighting.

Phase 06 · Month 14

Testing, snagging & handover

Systems testing, snagging closure, completion photographs, handover file and warranty documentation.

Six main categories: residential building construction, commercial construction, industrial construction, civil and infrastructure works, structural engineering, and renovation and redevelopment. Turnkey delivery and full project management are available across all of them.

A consultation starts with a site visit and a short brief covering your requirement, budget range, timeline and any drawings you already hold. We then come back with a written scope, an indicative estimate and a suggested programme, followed by a detailed proposal if you want to proceed.

Estimates are built elementally — foundation, structure, envelope, services, finishes and external works — so each line can be reviewed and adjusted. The estimate is paired with a cost plan and a cash-flow schedule, and variations are priced transparently before any work is instructed.

Duration depends on scale, approvals and ground conditions. A single residence is usually programmed across roughly 12 to 15 months, apartment and commercial blocks across 18 to 30 months, and industrial or infrastructure work is phased against operational requirements. Every proposal includes a milestone programme.

During construction: weekly written progress reports, photographic records, material and test documentation, and inspection sign-offs at each quality checkpoint. At handover you receive as-built drawings, test certificates, warranties, operation and maintenance manuals, and the completion photograph set.
